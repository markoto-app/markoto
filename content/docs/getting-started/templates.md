# Start from a template

The "New document" dialog has one choice besides the title: the
**Template**. A template is a starting text together with a design
that suits it: a report with a cover page and contents, a paper with
a title block, a runbook with numbered steps. Each card shows the
template's first page, so you see what you get before you create it.

::flow{id="start-from-a-template" hide-title}
::

## What just happened

You opened **Template** in the new-document dialog and picked a card.
The tabs sort the templates by who they are for: **Engineering**
(on-call runbook, design doc / RFC, API reference, project README),
**Academic** (arXiv preprint, IEEE conference paper, thesis, lecture
notes, APA essay), **Business** (report, business plan, client proposal,
one-pager, memo / letter, meeting notes) and **Writing** (novel
manuscript, newsletter, recipe). **All** lists them together with:

- **Demo**, picked for your first document: a short tour of markdown
  and of markoto (sharing, themes, comments, the AI, PDF export).
- **Blank**, the default after that: an empty page in your default
  design: the workspace's default theme when you create the document
  in a workspace that has one, otherwise markoto.
- **Resume**, a filled-in one-page CV for a made-up person that you
  rewrite line by line.

If you have saved designs of your own, each one appears as
**Blank in …** (also under **My designs**): an empty page in that design.

The other templates are skeletons: the headings the genre needs, the
tables, callouts and examples its design shows off, and short grey
comments saying what belongs in each section. The comments show in the
editor and to the AI; the preview and the PDF never show them. Where
the design prints authors, a subtitle or affiliations on the cover or
title block, the template fills in placeholders: replace them under
**Cover page** in the document theme.

Each template is written in the document language you pick under
**Details**: English or German, and Demo, Resume and Report also in
French, Spanish, Japanese and Chinese.

You can change any of it afterwards: the text is yours to edit, and the
design switches in the theme panel without touching the text.
