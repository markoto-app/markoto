# Compare markoto

markoto is a collaborative Markdown editor for documents that end up as pages: the preview shows printed pages as you type, and the PDF matches it. Here is how it compares with tools people often weigh it against. Each page lists where the other tool is better, and every fact links to its source.

## HackMD

HackMD is the more established tool for team notes, with slide mode and GitHub sync; markoto is for paged documents and PDFs, with no collaborator cap on the free plan and EU hosting.

[markoto vs HackMD: the full comparison](/compare/hackmd)

## HedgeDoc

HedgeDoc is AGPL open source, has slide mode and is less to run on your own server; markoto is source-available and needs more, but adds server-rendered PDF, print themes, workspaces, AI and a hosted EU option.

[markoto vs HedgeDoc: the full comparison](/compare/hedgedoc)

## Overleaf

Overleaf is the right tool for LaTeX, BibTeX and journal templates; markoto suits people who would rather write Markdown and still get a paged PDF, with collaborators on the free plan.

[markoto vs Overleaf: the full comparison](/compare/overleaf)

## Google Docs

Google Docs wins on familiarity, suggesting mode, offline use and citations; markoto keeps the text as Markdown, lays it out with print themes, and can be self-hosted or used on EU infrastructure.

[markoto vs Google Docs: the full comparison](/compare/google-docs)

## What markoto doesn't do

So you can rule it out quickly: markoto has no slide mode, no GitHub or Git sync, no citations or BibTeX, no suggestion or track-changes mode, no offline editing and no end-to-end encryption. It is source-available under the Functional Source License (FSL-1.1, Apache-2.0 two years after each release), not OSI open source.
