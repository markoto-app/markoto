# A one-page Markdown resume template

A resume written in Markdown is plain text you can keep in a folder, paste into an email or put under version control. What it usually lacks is a page. In markoto the preview shows the resume as an A4 page while you type, so you can see when it runs over, and the PDF has exactly that page.

## What's in it

- **Name and contact line.** Your name as the heading, then role, city, email, phone and one link on a single line.
- **Profile.** Two or three sentences on what you do and what you are looking for.
- **Experience.** One block per job, most recent first: role and company, place and dates, then two to four results with numbers.
- **Education, skills and languages.** Short lists, so the page has room for the experience.

Every section has a grey note in the source saying what belongs there. The notes never show on the page or in the PDF. The person in the template is made up; replace every line.

## Who it's for

People who would rather write their CV as text than fight a word processor's layout: designers, engineers, researchers, anyone applying in a field where a clean one-page resume is expected. It also suits keeping several versions, one per application, as separate documents.

## Keeping it on one page

The template comes with the **Resume** theme: Inter at 10 pt, small headings with a rule under them, 16 mm margins on A4, and no running header, page number or cover page. With that, the sample fills most of the page and leaves room for a few more lines.

- **Watch the page count.** The preview shows how many pages the document has. If it says 2, cut a line or two.
- **Shrink the type a little.** In the document theme, set the body text to 9.5 pt, or the margin to 12 mm.
- **Letter instead of A4.** Change the page size in the theme. Letter is shorter than A4, so check the page count again.
- **One page per job ad.** Duplicate the document (with an account) and shorten each copy for the job you apply for.
